# Phishing Awareness Guide

## What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack where an attacker pretends to be a
trusted person or organisation to trick someone into revealing information,
clicking a malicious link, opening an infected attachment, or making an
unauthorised payment.

## Common types of phishing

- **Email phishing:** Fake emails pretending to be from banks, employers, delivery companies, or IT support.
- **Spear phishing:** A targeted message using personal or work-related information.
- **Smishing:** Phishing through SMS or messaging apps.
- **Vishing:** Phishing through phone calls or voice messages.
- **Business Email Compromise (BEC):** Attackers impersonate executives, suppliers, or colleagues to request payments or sensitive data.

## Warning signs

- Urgent language such as “act immediately” or “your account will be closed.”
- Unexpected requests for passwords, one-time codes, payment details, or personal information.
- Slightly misspelled website addresses.
- Generic greetings such as “Dear customer.”
- Unexpected attachments, especially `.zip`, `.exe`, or macro-enabled documents.
- Links that do not match the organisation’s official website.
- Requests to bypass normal approval or security procedures.

## Safe response steps

1. Pause before clicking a link or opening an attachment.
2. Check the sender’s full email address, not only the display name.
3. Hover over links to check the destination website.
4. Verify unusual requests using a known phone number or official website.
5. Report the message to IT or the security team.
6. If you entered a password or clicked a suspicious link, change your password immediately and report it.

## Example

An employee receives an email claiming to be from the finance department. The
email says an invoice payment is overdue and asks the employee to approve a
bank transfer using a link.

The employee should not click the link. They should contact the finance
department using a known internal number and report the email to IT.
